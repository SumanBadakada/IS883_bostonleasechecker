## ag-move-in-costs | AG Guide: Move-in costs (M.G.L. c. 186 §15B(1)(b))
Before you move in, a landlord can ask you for no more than first month's rent, last month's rent,
a security deposit equal to one month's rent, and the cost of a new lock and key. Fees for pets,
applications, credit checks, cleaning or "move-in" are not allowed.

## ag-broker-fees | AG Guide: Broker fees (Massachusetts broker-fee law, effective Aug. 1, 2025)
Since August 1, 2025, the person who hires a real-estate broker pays that broker's fee. If the landlord
or the landlord's agent hired the broker, the landlord pays, and a lease or listing may not require the
tenant to pay it. A tenant pays a broker fee only if the tenant hired that broker. (Verify the exact
statutory citation and wording against the official guide.)

## ag-lead | AG Guide: Lead paint (M.G.L. c. 111 §197A; federal disclosure rule)
For housing built before 1978, the landlord must give the tenant the Tenant Lead Law Notification and
the Tenant Certification form, and disclose any known lead hazards and records, before the lease is
signed. A landlord may not refuse to rent, or end a tenancy, because a child under six will live in the
apartment and lead paint would need to be removed.

## ag-repairs | AG Guide: Repairs and the warranty of habitability
Every residential tenancy carries an implied warranty that the apartment is fit to live in and meets
the State Sanitary Code (105 CMR 410). The landlord must provide and maintain heat, hot water,
working plumbing, electricity, smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms, and freedom from rodents and insects
(in buildings of more than one unit). The tenant cannot waive this warranty in a lease. A tenant may be
made responsible only for damage the tenant or their guests caused.

## ag-heat-utilities | AG Guide: Heat and utilities (105 CMR 410)
From September 15 to June 15 the landlord must provide heat to at least the temperatures the State
Sanitary Code sets for day and night. The landlord must pay for heat, hot water and electricity unless
the apartment has its own separate meter and a written agreement says the tenant pays. A tenant cannot
be charged for water unless the landlord has installed low-flow fixtures and a separate submeter and the
lease says so in writing.

## ag-liability | AG Guide: Landlord liability (M.G.L. c. 186 §15)
A lease clause saying the landlord is not responsible for injury or damage caused by the landlord's own
negligence, or that the tenant will cover the landlord for such claims, is void and cannot be enforced.

## ag-attorney-fees | AG Guide: Attorney's fees (M.G.L. c. 186 §20)
If a lease says the tenant must pay the landlord's attorney's fees when the landlord goes to court,
the law gives the tenant the same right: the landlord must pay the tenant's attorney's fees if the
tenant wins or the landlord breaks the lease. A one-sided clause is still enforced both ways.

## ag-auto-renewal | AG Guide: Automatic renewal (M.G.L. c. 186 §15C)
A lease that renews automatically unless the tenant gives notice by a deadline cannot be enforced
against the tenant on that point unless the landlord reminded the tenant in writing, by certified mail
or in hand, between fifteen and thirty days before the tenant's notice deadline.

## ag-retaliation | AG Guide: Retaliation (M.G.L. c. 186 §18)
A landlord may not evict, raise rent or otherwise punish a tenant for reporting code violations,
withholding rent lawfully over bad conditions, or organising with other tenants. If the landlord acts
within six months of such a report, the law presumes retaliation.

## ag-eviction | AG Guide: Eviction (M.G.L. c. 186 §14; c. 239)
A landlord can remove a tenant only through a court eviction case. Changing the locks, shutting off
utilities, or removing a tenant's belongings to force them out is illegal, and a lease clause allowing
it is void. A lease cannot waive the tenant's right to a written notice to quit before an eviction case.

## ag-rent-increases | AG Guide: Rent increases during a lease
During a fixed-term lease the landlord may raise the rent only if the lease allows it (for example a
valid tax-escalation clause that meets M.G.L. c. 186 §15C). In a tenancy at will the landlord must give
written notice at least one full rental period (normally a month) before an increase takes effect.

## ag-discrimination | AG Guide: Discrimination (M.G.L. c. 151B)
A landlord may not refuse to rent, or set different lease terms, because of race, colour, religion,
national origin, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, age, ancestry, marital status, disability,
military status, receipt of public assistance or a rental voucher, or because the tenant has children.

## ag-subletting | AG Guide: Subletting, guests and ordinary lease rules
A lease may lawfully require the landlord's written consent before subletting, limit the number of
occupants to what the Sanitary Code allows, require renter's insurance, ban smoking, set quiet hours,
or restrict pets. These are common terms and are generally allowed.

## ag-condition-statement | AG Guide: Statement of condition (M.G.L. c. 186 §15B(2)(c))
If you pay a security deposit, the landlord must give you a written statement of the apartment's
condition within ten days. Note any damage on it and return it within fifteen days; keep a copy. This
protects your deposit when you move out.
